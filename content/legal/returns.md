Egyptian law gives you **two separate rights** when you buy from us online. They
cover different situations, they run for different lengths of time, and they
treat return shipping differently. This page explains both.

Both rights come from the Consumer Protection Law No. 181 of 2018 and its
Executive Regulations, issued by Prime Ministerial Decree No. 822 of 2019.

## Changing your mind — 14 days

You may return an item within **14 days of receiving it** without giving any
reason at all. The 14 days run from the day the item reaches you, not from the
day you placed the order.

To be accepted, the item must come back in its original condition — unworn,
unwashed, with its tags attached and its original packaging — and you should
have your receipt or other proof of purchase.

For a change-of-mind return, **you pay the cost of sending the item back.**

## Faulty, damaged or not as described — 30 days

This is a different right and a longer one. If an item is defective, arrives
damaged, is the wrong item, or does not match how it was described, you have
**30 days** to return or exchange it.

Here, **we pay the return shipping cost** once we have inspected the item and
confirmed the fault.

This right is not affected by the 14-day period above. An item that turns out
to be faulty on day 20 is still covered.

## Refunds

**Refunds are returned to you by the same method you paid with.** We cannot
substitute store credit or a voucher for a refund you are entitled to in money.

- **Paid by card** — the refund goes back to the card used for the order. After
  we issue it, your bank or card provider may take additional time to show it on
  your statement. That part is outside our control.
- **Paid cash on delivery** — there is no card to refund, so we will agree the
  arrangement with you directly: [COD REFUND METHOD].

Refunds are issued by our team once the returned item has been received and
checked. We will issue your refund within the period required by Egyptian law.

## Your rights under Egyptian law

Nothing on this page limits any right you have under Egyptian law. If a term
here ever conflicts with a mandatory legal protection, the law applies.
